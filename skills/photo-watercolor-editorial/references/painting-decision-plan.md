# Photo-Specific Painting Plan

Make this short internal plan after semantic eligibility and before writing the four prompt blocks. Start with **what must remain visible**: the protected first reading, any necessary second-read relation, face/contact/terminal evidence, and a user-required feature. Then choose only the painting decisions that help this particular photograph carry that reading. It is not copied verbatim into the prompt or treated as a second contract schema.

Before assigning paint behavior, settle the visible extent of each eligible region. Keep a short internal distinction between protected evidence, the simplified carrier that explains it, optional atmospheric color, and omitted construction. For each retained region, say what the viewer will actually see and where it stops. If removing a shape breaks the reading, retain its explanatory extent; if it only describes the setting, decide whether it earns an accent at all. Do not turn the entire background into an accent by default.

For the selected photograph, decide four things:

1. **Value structure:** when tonal grouping is needed, reduce the image to the few large light, middle, and dark shapes that clarify the first reading. Name the focal contrast only if it is useful, and reserve paper only where it supports the composition; neither a light field nor a dramatic shadow pattern is mandatory.
2. **Transparent relation:** only when the photograph contains a meaningful overlap, reflection, veil, or atmospheric crossing, describe the visible relation at that meeting: what remains readable and what stays behind, across, or around it. Describe the result, never an imagined physical pigment sequence.
3. **Edges:** assign only the edges needed for this image to remain recognizable. Keep decisive profile, contact, gaze, or terminal edges clear; soften secondary transitions; let only safe peripheral passages disappear into paper. A feather edge, fabric fold, rock contour, or cloud boundary earns definition only for its semantic role, not its category.
4. **Detail budget:** retain the few cues that establish category, pose, relation, material, or user-required evidence; omit noncontributing repeated units, texture, and construction. Dense foliage, feather pattern, fabric weave, branch networks, rock grain, grids, or architecture are never retained merely because they exist in the photograph.

Translate only the selected decisions into concrete scene language. Do not make all four headings, a fixed vocabulary, a stock light direction, transparent layering, or generous whitespace appear in every prompt. It should make a later visual review possible, but deterministic checks can establish only that a plan and compatible prompt meaning are present. Whether the generated image actually groups values, keeps overlaps readable, allocates edges, and omits texture requires visual or semantic review after generation.

## Make reduction visible

When environmental detail competes with the protected reading, describe its replacement rather than merely asking for less detail: a necessary support may become one quiet plane ending beyond the contact; atmospheric color may become a broad low-contrast wash fading into paper. Choose the extent from this photograph. A route, enclosure, continuous horizon, or support must remain continuous when that continuity explains the scene. Do not impose detached fragments, empty backgrounds, or a fixed paper percentage on every source.

For a fragmented primary, name the large connected light, middle, and dark regions and their relationship. Specify which recognition-bearing marks remain within them; let nonessential texture merge into the parent mass. A body should read as a volume before its surface pattern is noticed. Preserve diagnostic markings, face divisions, contact shadows, and supported terminals even when they interrupt a mass. Fewer sharp marks must not become a blurred face or a changed silhouette.

State what occupies the intended quiet field: open paper, a faint continuous tone, or a selected atmospheric wash. Pale miniature scenery is still scenery. Paper grain and pigment variation should remain subordinate to these larger shapes, not introduce a separate layer of high-contrast speckling.

Before compiling, compare the proposed plan with the protected reading: is any omitted construction being admitted again through an accent, support, or texture instruction? Is the proposed reduction actually visible in the described result? These are judgment questions, not keyword gates.
