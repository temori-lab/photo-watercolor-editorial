# Subject Lock, Relational Support, and Residual Trace Contract

Use this contract before geometry, design intervention, completeness, aspect ratio, face, or style. It selects literal content, recognizable support, and compressed traces.

## 1. Lock the subject

Select the smallest literal subject or relational group whose removal would collapse the intended reading. Preserve its reliable category, count, viewpoint, posture, event, relationship, complete envelope, and two to four recognition cues.

Keep only indispensable contact or support: the minimum plane, line, or broad shape required to explain resting, sitting, holding, carrying, containment, crossing, reflection, or another visible event. Do not preserve the support object's complete construction.

Large, sharp, central, colorful, repeated, or fully visible surroundings are not protected merely because they are easy to see. A single clear person or animal normally permits `editorial-recompose`; `source-locked` protects only the selected relational group, not the room or landscape.

## 2. Select relational support, then remove the residual field

Optionally keep **one relational-support family** when its recognizable category and around, behind, across, or enclosing placement explains the source, such as flowers around a person or foliage behind a bird. Keep a few readable outer shapes, uneven rhythm, broad color, and placement; compress count, repetition, texture, perspective, branch networks, and depth. It has no completeness or safe-area protection and stays quieter than the primary, but must remain recognizable.

Temporarily remove every other object, surface, reflection, fixture, plant, architectural fragment, and photographic edge artifact. Judge the remaining primary-plus-support design as a whole rather than classifying every leftover object.

Ask one question:

> Without the removed context, would this become a generic illustration that could fit almost any similar subject?

If no, omit the residual field completely. If yes, restore only source-specific compositional memory through the trace budget below.

## 3. Apply the trace budget

Restore at most:

- **one structural trace**: one to three broken or softened lines that retain a viewing boundary, spatial layer, counter-axis, interval, or framing pressure;
- **one atmospheric trace**: one broad wash or one sparse related rhythm that retains source-specific light, place, season, or palette.

A trace survives only when all are true:

1. it makes the poster less generic;
2. it compresses into no more than three marks or one broad wash/rhythm;
3. it no longer reads as a complete secondary object;
4. it remains clearly subordinate to the subject and does not demand its own focal detail.

Otherwise omit it. Do not restore exact object count, construction, perspective, repeated grids, room inventory, dense foliage, or full scenic depth. A trace is not permission to reconstruct the source scene.

Examples:

- A reclining person keeps a pale contact plane; window bars may survive as up to three broken horizontal strokes when their layering makes the image source-specific. Curtains, bed construction, table, bottle, reflection, and bedding detail disappear.
- A bird may keep several readable leaves behind it and three interrupted window lines; dense branches and reflections disappear.
- A person may keep reduced recognizable flowers around the figure and, only for a distinct function, one sky wash; exact count and meadow depth disappear.

## Internal record

```text
primary: [smallest literal subject/group + recognition cues]
support: [minimum event-bearing plane, line, or broad shape]
relational_support: [none / one recognizable family + relation + compressed cues]
generic_without_context: [yes/no + short reason]
structural_trace: [none / up to three marks + function]
atmospheric_trace: [none / one broad wash or sparse rhythm + function]
drawable_scope: [positive content sent to generation]
```

Keep discarded object names out of the image prompt. Describe the selected subject, support, relational-support family, traces, and clean ivory paper through positive visual results. End with `Show only the selected subject, essential support, and open paper.`

## Validation

Reject or revise when the primary loses identity, action, relationship, or face behavior; support becomes a second subject; relational support becomes anonymous or dominant; the result becomes generic; a trace reconstructs background objects; or removed surroundings regain contours and perspective.
